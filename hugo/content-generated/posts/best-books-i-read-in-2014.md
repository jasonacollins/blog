<p>Continuing my tradition of giving the best books I read in the year - generally released in other years - the best books I read in 2014 are below (albeit from a smaller pool than usual).</p>
<ul>
<li><p>John Coates’s (2012)  <a href="/posts/the-biology-of-boom-and-bust.html">The Hour Between Dog and Wolf: Risk Taking, Gut Feelings and the Biology of Boom and Bust</a>: The best book I read this year. An innovative consideration of how human biology affects financial decision making.</p></li>
<li><p>Gregory Clark’s (2013) <a href="/posts/the-genetic-basis-of-social-mobility.html">The Son Also Rises: Surnames and the History of Social Mobility</a>: Not the most exciting book to read, but an important examination of social mobility.</p></li>
<li><p>David Colander and Roland Kupers’s (2014) <a href="/posts/complexity-and-the-art-of-public-policy.html">Complexity and the Art of Public Policy</a>: An important way to think about policy, even though I’m not convinced by many of their proposed applications.</p></li>
<li><p>Gerd Gigerenzer’s (2010) <em>Rationality for Mortals: How People Cope with Uncertainty</em>: Essays capturing a lot of Gigerenzer’s important work. I reread it following his disappointing <a href="/posts/nudging-citizens-to-be-risk-savvy.html">Risk Savvy</a>. I didn’t write a review, but posted about parts of the book <a href="/posts/four-perspectives-on-human-decision-making.html">here</a>, <a href="/posts/the-power-of-heuristics.html">here</a> and <a href="/posts/the-unrealistic-assumptions-of-biology.html">here</a>.</p></li>
<li><p>Sendhil Mullainathan and Eldar Shafir’s (2013) <a href="/posts/scarcity-of-time-money-friends-and-bandwidth.html">Scarcity: Why Having Too Little Means So Much</a>: A novel way of looking at scarcity that extends beyond the typical analysis in economics, but I’m not convinced it presents a coherent new perspective on how the world works.</p></li>
<li><p>P.G. Wodehouse’s (1936) Young Men in Spats: Magic.</p></li>
</ul>

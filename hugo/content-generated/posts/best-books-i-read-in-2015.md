<p>A touch late, but as for earlier years, my list comprises the best books I read in the year, not the best of those released in the year (in fact, almost every book I read was released before 2015). It’s a short list - I read only 20 or so non-fiction books this year - but here it is:</p>
<p><em>Triumph of the City: How Our Greatest Invention Makes Us Richer, Smarter, Greener, Healthier, and Happier</em> by Edward Glaeser. A strong message for environmentalists.</p>
<p><a href="/posts/millers-spent-sex-evolution-and-consumer-behavior.html">Spent</a> by Geoffrey Miller. I included this book in my <a href="/posts/best-books-i-read-in-2011.html">2011 list</a>, but read it again this year in preparation for a presentation I was giving. It deserves to be listed again. Evolutionary psychology at its most readable.</p>
<p><a href="/posts/manzis-uncontrolled.html">Uncontrolled: The Surprising Payoff of Trial-and-Error for Business, Politics, and Society</a> by James Manzi. It should be read by every social scientist.</p>
<p>I read a bunch of long classics, including Tolstoy’s War and Peace and Anna Karenina, and Conrad’s Nostromo (fantastic). One strong recommendation is Richard Flanagan’s <em>The Narrow Road to the Deep North</em>.</p>
<p>As an aside, I also re-read Daniel Kahneman’s <a href="/posts/kahnemans-thinking-fast-and-slow.html">Thinking, Fast and Slow</a>. I included it in my <a href="/posts/the-best-books-i-read-in-2012.html">2012 list</a>, and my rating of it has dropped. The last four years haven’t been kind to some sections. (I posted about this <a href="/posts/re-reading-kahnemans-thinking-fast-and-slow.html">here</a>).</p>
<p>I’ve got a dozen or so sets of notes from books I read last year that I hope to turn into posts in the near future - including for <em>The Triumph of the City</em>, Kenrick and Griskevicius’s <a href="/posts/kenrick-and-griskeviciuss-the-rational-animal.html">The Rational Animal</a>, Saad’s <em>The Consuming Instinct</em>, Thiel’s <a href="/posts/thiels-zero-to-one.html">Zero to One</a>, Levitin’s <em>The Organized Mind</em> and Taleb’s <em>Antifragile</em>.</p>
<p>And although I’ve got copies, I haven’t yet read Garett Jones’s <a href="/posts/joness-hive-mind-how-your-nations-iq-matters-so-much-more-than-your-own.html">Hive Mind</a>, Joe Henrich’s <a href="/posts/henrichs-the-secret-of-our-success-how-culture-is-driving-human-evolution-domesticating-our-species-and-making-us-smarter.html">The Secret of Our Success</a> or Greg Ip’s <a href="/posts/ips-foolproof-why-safety-can-be-dangerous-and-how-danger-makes-us-safe.html">Foolproof</a> (although I have been giving out the first two of these as Christmas presents).</p>

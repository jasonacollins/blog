<p>The best books I <em>read</em> in 2016 - generally released in other years - are below (in no particular order). For the non-fiction books, the links lead to my reviews.</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Joe Henrich’s <a href="/posts/henrichs-the-secret-of-our-success-how-culture-is-driving-human-evolution-domesticating-our-species-and-making-us-smarter.html">The Secret of Our Success: How Culture Is Driving Human Evolution, Domesticating Our Species, and Making Us Smarter</a> (2015): A lot of interesting ideas, but left me with a lot of questions.</p></li>
<li><p>Garett Jones’s <a href="/posts/joness-hive-mind-how-your-nations-iq-matters-so-much-more-than-your-own.html">Hive Mind: How Your Nation’s IQ Matters So Much More Than Your Own</a> (2015): A fantastic exposition of some important but neglected features of the world.</p></li>
<li><p>Phil Rosenzweig’s <a href="/posts/rosenzweigs-left-brain-right-stuff-how-leaders-make-winning-decisions.html">Left Brain, Right Stuff: How Leaders Make Winning Decisions</a> (2014): An entertaining examination of how behavioural economics findings hold up for real world decision making.</p></li>
<li><p>Philip Tetlock’s <a href="/posts/tetlocks-expert-political-judgment-how-good-is-it-how-can-we-know.html">Expert Political Judgment: How Good Is It? How Can We Know?</a> (2006): I re-read this before reading Tetlock’s also good <a href="/posts/tetlock-and-gardners-superforecasting-the-art-and-science-of-prediction.html">Superforecasting</a>, but Expert Political Judgement is the superior of the two books.</p></li>
<li><p>Jonathan Last’s <a href="/posts/lasts-what-to-expect-when-no-ones-expecting-americas-coming-demographic-disaster.html">What to Expect When No One’s Expecting: America’s Coming Demographic Disaster</a> (2014): Much to disagree or argue with, but entertaining and a lot to like.</p></li>
<li><p>John Kay’s <a href="/posts/kays-other-peoples-money.html">Other People’s Money</a> (2015): A fantastic critique of the modern financial system and regulation.</p></li>
<li><p>Fyodor Dostoevsky’s <a href="http://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2554">Crime and Punishment</a>: The classic I most enjoyed.</p></li>
</ul>

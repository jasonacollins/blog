<p>Yesterday was the second edition of the <a href="http://www.msix.com.au/">Marketing Science Ideas Xchange (MSiX)</a>. It was a more eclectic set of speakers than last year, extending from the first year’s behavioural economics focus to include neuroscience and “big data”. In my mind, the increased variety worked well.</p>
<p>If you saw <a href="/posts/please-not-another-bias-an-evolutionary-take-on-behavioural-economics.html">yesterday’s post</a>, I spoke at the event and provided a reading list which included Matt Ridley’s <em>The Red Queen</em>, Geoffrey Miller’s <a href="/posts/millers-spent-sex-evolution-and-consumer-behavior.html">Spent</a>, Gad Saad’s <a href="/posts/saads-the-evolutionary-bases-of-consumption.html">The Evolutionary Bases of Consumption</a> and <em>The Consuming Instinct</em>, Amotz Zahavi’s <em>The Handicap Principle</em>, Robert Frank’s <a href="/posts/franks-luxury-fever.html">Luxury Fever</a>, Gerd Gigerenzer’s <a href="/posts/gerd-gigerenzers-rationality-for-mortals-how-people-cope-with-uncertainty.html">Rationality for Mortals</a> and Douglas Kenrick and Vlad Griskevicius’s <a href="/posts/kenrick-and-griskeviciuss-the-rational-animal.html">The Rational Animal</a>.</p>
<p>From the other presentations, I didn’t record all the books as they were mentioned, but here are the main titles that I can recall:</p>
<p>Michael Norton’s <em>Happy Money: The Science of Smarter Spending</em></p>
<p>Suzanne Mettler’s <em>The Submerged State: How Invisible Government Policies Undermine American Democracy</em></p>
<p>Robert Cialdini’s <em>Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion</em></p>
<p>Clayton Christensen’s <em>The Innovator’s Dilemma: The Revolutionary Book That Will Change the Way You Do Business</em></p>
<p>Andrew Leigh’s <em>The Economics of Just About Everything</em></p>
<p>Byron Sharp’s <em>How Brands Grow: What Marketers Don’t Know</em></p>
<p>Adam Ferrier’s <em>The Advertising Effect: How to Change Behaviour</em></p>
<p>Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein’s <a href="/posts/thaler-and-sunsteins-nudge.html">Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness</a></p>
<p>James Hurman’s <em>The Case for Creativity</em> (which seems to have drifted out of print)</p>

<p>This week we have paleo for dogs, irrational consumers and the question of what is the right size:</p>
<ol type="1">
<li><p><a href="http://johnhawks.net/weblog/reviews/genomics/dogs/dog-selection-starch-axelsson-2013.html">John Hawks posts</a> on a <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/nature11837">new Nature paper</a> that provides a reason for “why we feed dogs kibble instead of raw beef” - well, at least those who don’t have their dogs on the paleo diet. Like humans with a long agricultural history, dogs have extensive duplication of the amylase gene, which assists in the digestion of starch. <a href="http://blogs.discovermagazine.com/gnxp/2013/01/as-man-is-the-dog-is">Razib asks</a> “Is Paleolithic man to Holocene man what the wolf is to the dog?”</p></li>
<li><p><a href="http://www.theatlantic.com/business/archive/2013/01/the-irrational-consumer-why-economics-is-dead-wrong-about-how-we-make-choices/267255/">The Atlantic covers</a> a <a href="http://www.nber.org/papers/w18687">new paper by Daniel McFadden</a> that summarises the case against the neoclassical economic view of the consumer. From a quick skim, there’s not too many surprises but it’s a nice summary.</p></li>
<li><p><a href="http://twitter.com/NGruen1">Nick Gruen</a> tweeted a link to this 1928 classic by J.B.S. Haldane, <a href="http://irl.cs.ucla.edu/papers/right-size.html">On Being the Right Size</a>. Andy Haldane of the Bank of England borrowed it for <a href="http://www.bankofengland.co.uk/publications/Documents/speeches/2012/speech615.pdf">a speech on bank size</a>. I suggest going <a href="http://irl.cs.ucla.edu/papers/right-size.html">back to the original</a>.</p></li>
</ol>

<p>More like a month of links, but here goes:</p>
<ol type="1">
<li><p>We’re going to be hearing a lot about Greg Clark’s new book on social mobility - <a href="/posts/the-genetic-basis-of-social-mobility.html">The Son Also Rises: Surnames and the History of Social Mobility</a>. <a href="http://opinionator.blogs.nytimes.com/2014/02/21/your-fate-thank-your-ancestors/">Clark gives a synopsis in the NYT</a>. In short, Clark and his colleagues estimate “that 50 to 60 percent of variation in overall status is determined by your lineage.”</p></li>
<li><p>Kolk and colleagues present a paper in <a href="https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2013.2561">The Proceedings of the Royal Society B</a> that presents a model in which intergenerational fertility correlations drive a long-term fertility increase. They cite my <a href="/posts/fertility-is-going-to-go-up.html">working paper</a> as a source for genetic correlations driving fertility up. When (if) I get that paper published, do I now cite back?</p></li>

<li><p><a href="http://www.latimes.com/opinion/commentary/la-oe-chabris-google-intelligence-20140309,0,7897686.story">Don’t hire like Google</a>.</p></li>

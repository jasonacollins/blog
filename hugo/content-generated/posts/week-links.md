<p>Links this week:</p>
<ol type="1">
<li><p>David Sloan Wilson and Jonathan Haidt have kicked off <a href="http://www.forbes.com/sites/darwinatwork/">an evolution and business blog at Forbes</a>. It will be worth a read, and unsurprisingly <a href="http://www.forbes.com/sites/darwinsbusiness/2013/09/05/sears-ignores-the-invisible-band/">the first post</a> reflects Wilson and Haidt’s group selection leanings. It should give plenty of fodder for interesting posts. I’ve written about <a href="/posts/haidts-group-selection.html">Haidt’s group selection views before</a>, plus plenty of posts on Wilson’s (<a href="/posts/economic-cosmology-the-rational-egotistical-individual.html">here</a> and <a href="/posts/economic-cosmology-the-invisible-hand.html">here</a> for starters).</p></li>
<li><p>Also on Jonathan Haidt, <a href="http://www.thisviewoflife.com/index.php/magazine/articles/welcome-to-the-new-business-section">he is the editor of a new business section</a> at Evolution: This View of Life.</p></li>
<li><p>A book that looks worth a read: <a href="http://rationalanimalbook.com/">The Rational Animal</a>. <a href="http://www.psychologytoday.com/blog/sex-murder-and-the-meaning-life/201308/are-you-smarter-you-think">Doug Kenrick posts on it</a>.</p></li>
<li><p><a href="http://www.ft.com/intl/cms/s/2/afc5377e-1026-11e3-a258-00144feabdc0.html#axzz2dUvfRkUI">Tim Harford’s article on Lin Ostrom’s work</a> is good. Ostrom gets quoted a lot for her suggestion that a single international agreement to deal with climate change would be a mistake, less so for her suggestion of polycentric action at all levels - just the sort of thing that would have the typical economist decrying as horribly inefficient.</p></li>

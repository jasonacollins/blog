<p>Links this week:</p>
<ol type="1">
<li><p>A week ago <a href="/posts/social-mobility-across-the-generations.html">I posted</a> about a <a href="http://www.economist.com/news/finance-and-economics/21571399-surnames-offer-depressing-clues-extent-social-mobility-over">piece in the Economist</a> on Greg Clark’s work using surnames to estimate social mobility. A debate followed the Economist article, with contributions by <a href="http://www.economist.com/blogs/freeexchange/2013/02/mobility-0">Miles Corak</a>, <a href="http://www.economist.com/blogs/freeexchange/2013/02/mobility-1">Francisco Ferreira</a>, <a href="http://www.economist.com/blogs/freeexchange/2013/02/mobility-2">Greg Clark</a> (<a href="http://www.economist.com/blogs/freeexchange/2013/02/mobility-3">and again</a>), and <a href="http://www.economist.com/blogs/freeexchange/2013/02/mobility-4">Jason Long</a>. All are worth reading.</p></li>
<li><p><a href="http://jonathanlast.com/childlessness-happiness-and-fertility-increases/">Jonathan Last notes</a> three issues that he would include in a second edition of <a href="/posts/lasts-what-to-expect-when-no-ones-expecting-americas-coming-demographic-disaster.html">What to Expect When No One’s Expecting: America’s Coming Demographic Disaster</a>. The third relates to <a href="/posts/fertility-is-going-to-go-up.html">my recent working paper</a> with Oliver Richards on natural selection driving an increase in fertility.</p></li>
<li><p>The anthropology wars are in full swing at the moment, moving from Diamond to Chagnon. The two most interesting articles I read this week were by <a href="http://www.theatlantic.com/health/archive/2013/02/sex-lies-and-separating-science-from-ideology/273169/">Alice Dreger in the Atlantic</a> and <a href="http://blogs.scientificamerican.com/cross-check/2013/02/18/the-weird-irony-at-the-heart-of-the-napoleon-chagnon-affair/">John Horgan in Scientific American</a>. I’d be interested in hearing a response from the gang of five to Horgan’s claims.</p></li>
<li><p>Half a year late, I have finally read <a href="http://www.newrepublic.com/article/politics/magazine/103952/happyism-deirdre-mccloskey-economics-happiness#">this article by Deirdre McCloskey</a> on the measurement of happiness. It’s full of straw men, but many passages are fantastic.</p></li>
</ol>

<p>Better late than never….</p>
<p>The best books I <em>read</em> in 2019 - generally released in other years - are below. Where I have reviewed, the link leads to that review (not many reviews this year).</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Nick Chater, <a href="/posts/nick-chaters-the-mind-is-flat-the-illusion-of-mental-depth-and-the-improvised-mind.html"><em>The Mind is Flat</em></a>: A great book in which Chater argues that there are no ‘hidden depths’ to our minds.</p></li>
<li><p>Stephan Guyenet, <em>The Hungry Brain: Outsmarting the Instincts that Make us Overeat</em>: Excellent summary of modern nutrition research and how the body “regulates” its weight.</p></li>
<li><p>Jonathan Morduch and Rachel Schneider, <em>The Financial Diaries: How American Families Cope in a World of Uncertainty</em>: I find a lot of value reading about the world outside of my bubble. I learnt a lot from this book.</p></li>
<li><p>Paul Seabright, <em>The Company of Strangers</em>: An excellent exploration of the evolutionary foundations of cooperation. A staple of my <a href="/posts/economics_and_evolutionary_biology_reading_list.html">evolutionary biology and economics reading list</a>.</p></li>
<li><p>Lenore Skenazy, <em>Free-Range Kids: How to Raise Safe, Self-Reliant Children (Without Going Nuts with Worry)</em>: A fun read of some wise advice.</p></li>
<li><p>M Mitchell Waldrop, <em>The Dream Machine: J.C.R. Licklider and the Revolution That Made Computing Personal</em>: A bit too much detail, but a worthwhile story about the origins of personal computing. Many of the concepts about human-machine interaction remain relevant today.</p></li>
</ul>
<p>Below is the full list of books that I read in 2019 (with links where reviewed and starred if a re-read). The volume of my reading declined year-on-year again, with 61 books total (40 non-fiction, 21 fiction). Most of that decline came in the back half of the year when I spent a lot of time reading and researching around some narrow academic topics. 45 of the below were read before June. I could add a lot of children’s books to the list (especially Enid Blyton), but I’ll leave those aside.</p>

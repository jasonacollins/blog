<p>The 2015 Marketing Science Ideas Xchange - MSiX - <a href="http://mumbrella.com.au/harvard-business-schools-michael-norton-to-headline-mumbrellas-marketing-science-conference-msix-299347">has been announced</a> for 30 July in Sydney. As it says <a href="http://www.msix.com.au/">in the blurb</a>, MSiX “is dedicated to exploring how brands can benefit from the interface between behavioural science and marketing.”</p>
<p>The headline speaker is <a href="http://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/profile.aspx?facId=326229">Michael Norton</a>, Harvard professor, author of <em>Happy Money: The Science of Happier Spending</em> and developer of the first set of experiments on the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IKEA_effect">IKEA effect</a> (that last point is the reason I knew his name when I heard he would be speaking).</p>
<p>The rest of the speakers and further detail on the conference are <a href="http://www.msix.com.au/speakers/">here</a> - with the speaking line-up including me. I’ll be talking about how behavioural economics (science) could benefit from a good dose of evolutionary biology, and how that evolutionary lens can be valuable in understanding consumer behaviour.</p>
<p>Last year’s event - headlined by Rory Sutherland (who linked me into this MSiX world) - was a pretty good day, and this year looks promising too.</p>

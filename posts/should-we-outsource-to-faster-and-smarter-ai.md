Post - reflection on Krook's When Autonomy Breaks: The Hidden Existential Risk of AI

Krook's dealing with the counterarguments are battles against strawmen.

First, he argues against those who see outsourcing as a good thing. If self-driving cars are much safer and human error causes most aaccidents, should humans have an *obligation* to stop driving?

Krook takes this to the "logical conclusion" (his words) of placing restrictions on individual liberty. A self-driving car might refuse to take you to your friend's birthday party on a rainy Sunday night.

There are a lot of ideas hidden in this argument. What is the balance between harms to self versus harms to others? What are the externalities?

This becomes more obvious when Krook asks rhetorically if we should insist on humans flying place for themselves. Are we harming the pilot's autonomy by not allowing her to fly the plane? Should we do away with autopilot?

Krook also addresses the argument of AGI as a form of conservatorship. If someone is unable to make their own life decisions, courts might appoint a guardian.

The two examples pulled out in this instance are Britney Spears being placed under the guardianship by her father for 13 years.

The difference, of course, is that Britney did not choose to hand over power. Nor was she able to simply take it back when she so desired. There was a line that voluntary can become involuntary (citing an article on the Britney case), but ...

Deskilling - I think this is a much more real risk - but the question becomes which skills are vital. I can't shoe a horse, yet that skill is suitably no longer part or my repertoire. Which skills should we maintain?

Part of the decay to knowledge recall - OK with that?

Deskilling in some domains - upskilling in others?
